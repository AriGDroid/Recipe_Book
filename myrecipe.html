Recipe for Funeral Potatoes

Jump to Ingredients Jump to Instructions Jump to Nutrition Information Jump to Tips

Recipe Name: Funeral Potatoes

A delicious and easy-to-make side dish that's perfect for any occasion.



Ingredients:

  • 2 lbs frozen hash brown potatoes, thawed
  • 2 cups sour cream
  • 1 can cream of chicken soup
  • 6 tablespoons melted butter (plus 1/2 cup for the topping)
  • 2 cups shredded cheddar cheese
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 teaspoon dried minced onion
  • 2 1/2 cups crushed cornflakes


Instructions:

  1. Heat your oven to 350°F and spray a 9x13-inch baking pan with non-stick cooking spray.
  2. Mix the sour cream, cream of chicken soup, 6 tablespoons of melted butter, salt, pepper, and dried onion in a large bowl.
  3. Stir in the hash browns and 2 cups of shredded cheddar cheese.
  4. Spread the potato mixture evenly into your prepared baking pan.
  5. Mix the remaining 1/2 cup of melted butter with the crushed cornflakes, then sprinkle it over the top of the potatoes.
  6. Bake for 40 to 45 minutes until the top is golden and the edges are bubbly
  7. Remove from oven and let it cool for a few minutes before serving. Enjoy!

Tips and Tricks:

Potato Prep and Texture

  • Decide whether to thaw your hash browns completely or keep them frozen based on your preferred method, but always drain excess liquid so the casserole does not get watery.
  • Use shredded hash browns for a softer, more uniform texture, or use diced or cubed hash browns if you prefer a heartier bite.
  • Shred blocks of sharp cheddar cheese yourself instead of buying pre-bagged shreds; fresh cheese melts much smoother and avoids anti-caking additives.

Enhancing Flavor and Toppings

  • Always mix your crushed cornflakes, Ritz crackers, or potato chips with melted butter before adding them to the top so they toast evenly and stay crunchy.
  • While classic cornflakes are traditional, you can easily substitute crushed Ritz crackers, potato chips, or even Cheez-Its for a fun twist.
  • Stir in diced ham, cooked bacon bits, or shredded rotisserie chicken to turn this classic side dish into a complete, hearty meal.

Make-Ahead and Baking Tips

  • Assemble the potato and sauce mixture in your baking dish up to 24 hours ahead of time, cover it tightly, and store it in the fridge.
  • If making ahead, keep the buttered cereal topping off until right before it goes into the oven to prevent it from getting soggy.
  • Pop the baked casserole under the broiler for 1 to 2 minutes at the very end of cooking to get a deeply golden-brown crust.
